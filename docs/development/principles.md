# Project and collaboration principles

Filebonsai should be fun to build, useful enough to maintain, and strong public
evidence of polished product delivery plus backend engineering. High-quality code,
explicit guarantees, and inspectable recovery behavior matter more than adding a long
feature list. Do not frame the product as finance software.

Prefer idiomatic Java/Spring and a real code-first OpenAPI-to-generated-client system.
Explain consequential choices through the implemented work; do not require artificial
hand-written exercises as a prerequisite. Keep project guidance focused on reproducible
engineering decisions rather than contributor background or private context.

Favor concrete, inspectable outputs and concise mobile-readable summaries. Continue
from verified repository state rather than reopening settled stack/product choices.
Routine in-scope work does not need repeated confirmation, but publishing, paid
infrastructure, credentials, destructive production actions, and scope expansion do.

Keep collaboration terse and useful. Do not repeat the request, narrate routine tool
work, or turn each passing check into a separate status update. Report a decision,
blocker, meaningful milestone, or final result; combine routine detail into the final
handoff when it helps assess the outcome.

Choose verification proportionally before running it. Ordinary checks belong in the
workspace sandbox while building. Batch every required elevated check into one final
request, avoiding overlapping runs whose only purpose is duplicate certification.
Repeat an elevated request only after a repair or scope change makes a new result
necessary.

Public claims stay proportional to evidence. Open source alone does not prove demand;
tests alone do not prove usefulness; mockups do not prove an application; an emulator
does not prove cloud compatibility; and a successful build does not prove recovery.
